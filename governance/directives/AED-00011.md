# AED-00011

# Compliance & Enforcement Architecture

---

# Status

**Proposed**

Normative.

---

# 1. Purpose

This AED defines the Enforcement Subsystem.

Governance without enforcement is aspiration.

The repository SHALL treat compliance verification as a first-class architectural concern.

Enforcement SHALL be deterministic, measurable, and self-describing.

---

# 2. Motivation

The governance model established by AED-00001 through AED-00010 defines:

* what SHALL exist;
* what SHALL NOT exist;
* how entities SHALL behave;
* how knowledge SHALL flow.

None of those documents define how to verify those expectations are met.

Without verification, governance degrades silently.

Entropy accumulates invisibly.

Anti-patterns reappear without detection.

This AED closes that gap.

---

# 3. Core Principle

Governance defines expectations.

Enforcement verifies them.

These are distinct responsibilities.

They SHALL remain architecturally separated.

Enforcement SHALL never redefine governance.

Governance SHALL never depend upon enforcement implementation.

---

# 4. Compliance Definition

Compliance is the measurable degree to which repository state conforms to governance expectations.

Compliance is not binary.

Compliance is a spectrum.

The repository SHALL maintain a Compliance Score as its canonical health indicator.

---

# 5. Enforcement Philosophy

Enforcement SHALL be:

* automated wherever possible;
* deterministic;
* traceable;
* non-destructive;
* self-describing.

Enforcement SHALL NOT:

* modify governance;
* modify architectural knowledge;
* produce false negatives silently;
* require human intervention for schema-level checks.

---

# 6. Enforcement Layers

Enforcement SHALL be decomposed into five independent layers.

Layers execute in ascending order.

Each layer depends upon the layers below it.

---

## Layer 0

### Schema Enforcement

Purpose:

Verify that every artifact conforms to its declared format.

Checks include:

* required metadata fields are present;
* identifier format is valid;
* version follows semantic versioning;
* status is a recognized lifecycle value;
* depends_on references existing identifiers;
* review_cycle is declared.

Layer 0 is the prerequisite for all subsequent layers.

An artifact failing Layer 0 SHALL be treated as structurally invalid.

---

## Layer 1

### Structural Enforcement

Purpose:

Verify that architectural relationships are consistent.

Checks include:

* dependency graphs contain no cycles;
* higher-stability layers do not depend upon lower-stability layers;
* parent references resolve to existing entities;
* superseded_by references are bidirectional;
* every AED in depends_on is in an accepted or published status.

Layer 1 verifies that the architecture is coherent as a system.

---

## Layer 2

### Invariant Enforcement

Purpose:

Verify that declared invariants are satisfied.

Invariants are defined across the governance system.

Examples:

* INV-001: Knowledge SHALL be canonical. (AED-00003)
* GOV-001: Governance SHALL remain centralized. (AED-00005)
* CAP-002: Every Capability SHALL expose one primary responsibility. (AED-00006)
* CON-001: Every Entity SHALL expose one Contract. (AED-00007)

Invariant enforcement SHALL produce one of three outcomes per invariant:

```text
PASS     — invariant satisfied
WARN     — invariant at risk; documented exception exists
FAIL     — invariant violated; no exception registered
```

Layer 2 is the primary mechanism for detecting governance drift.

---

## Layer 3

### Semantic Enforcement

Purpose:

Verify that vocabulary and ontological consistency are preserved.

Checks include:

* no new concepts introduced without AED review;
* capability specializations remain within the hierarchy defined in AED-00006;
* knowledge artifacts do not redefine governance;
* runtime artifacts do not persist into permanent knowledge;
* terminology matches AED-00003 definitions.

Semantic enforcement detects vocabulary drift before it becomes structural degradation.

---

## Layer 4

### Lifecycle Enforcement

Purpose:

Verify that all entities maintain valid lifecycle states.

Checks include:

* no entity exists without a declared status;
* status transitions follow the defined lifecycle;
* deprecated entities expose a superseded_by reference;
* archived entities are not consumed by active capabilities;
* review_cycle deadlines are tracked and surfaced.

Lifecycle enforcement ensures the repository does not accumulate stale or orphaned artifacts.

---

# 7. Enforcement Modes

Every enforcement check SHALL operate in one of three modes.

---

## Hard

The check represents a blocking violation.

Execution SHALL be prevented.

Hard violations MUST be resolved before repository work continues.

Examples:

* schema validation failure on a governance document;
* dependency cycle in the AED graph;
* critical invariant failure with no registered exception.

---

## Soft

The check represents a degradation risk.

Execution MAY continue.

Soft violations MUST be reported and tracked.

Examples:

* missing recommended metadata field;
* invariant at risk with an active exception registered;
* lifecycle deadline approaching.

---

## Advisory

The check represents an opportunity for improvement.

Execution continues normally.

Advisory findings SHOULD be reviewed periodically.

Examples:

* semantic vocabulary drift observed;
* knowledge artifact approaching review_cycle threshold;
* capability without optional contract fields.

---

# 8. Compliance Score

The repository SHALL maintain one canonical Compliance Score.

The score represents the overall conformance state of the repository.

---

## Calculation Model

```text
Compliance Score = Σ( Layer Weight × Layer Score ) / Σ( Layer Weights )
```

Recommended layer weights:

| Layer | Name             | Weight |
| ----- | ---------------- | ------ |
| L0    | Schema           | 30     |
| L1    | Structural       | 25     |
| L2    | Invariant        | 25     |
| L3    | Semantic         | 10     |
| L4    | Lifecycle        | 10     |

The Compliance Score SHALL range from 0.0 to 1.0.

---

## Score Interpretation

| Score        | Status       | Meaning                                          |
| ------------ | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 0.95 – 1.00  | HEALTHY      | Repository fully conforms to governance.         |
| 0.80 – 0.94  | ACCEPTABLE   | Minor degradations present; remediation advised. |
| 0.60 – 0.79  | DEGRADED     | Significant violations; remediation required.    |
| 0.00 – 0.59  | CRITICAL     | Governance integrity compromised.                |

A CRITICAL score SHALL trigger an architectural review.

---

## Score Visibility

The Compliance Score SHALL be:

* computed on every enforcement run;
* exposed in the Repository Registry;
* visible in the Repository Boot sequence;
* included in every architectural review.

---

# 9. Violation Model

Every enforcement failure is a Violation.

---

## Violation Record

Every Violation SHALL expose:

* Identifier
* Layer
* Mode (Hard / Soft / Advisory)
* Affected Entity
* Violated Rule (invariant ID, layer check, schema field)
* Detected At (timestamp)
* Description
* Suggested Remediation
* Exception Reference (if applicable)
* Status (Open / Resolved / Excepted)

---

## Violation Registry

The repository SHALL maintain a Violation Registry.

The Violation Registry SHALL:

* enumerate all open violations;
* preserve resolved violations for historical reference;
* link violations to exceptions;
* be discoverable through the Repository Registry.

The Violation Registry SHALL NOT be treated as a runtime artifact.

It is a governance artifact.

---

# 10. Exception Model

Not every governance violation is remediable immediately.

The repository SHALL support explicit exceptions.

---

## Exception Record

Every exception SHALL expose:

* Identifier
* Violated Rule
* Justification
* Scope (which entities are affected)
* Duration (expiry date or condition)
* Owner (human accountable)
* Exit Criteria (how the exception is closed)
* Review Date

---

## Exception Rules

Exceptions SHALL be explicit.

Implicit exceptions SHALL NOT exist.

Every exception SHALL have an expiry condition.

Perpetual exceptions SHALL be considered architectural debt.

Exceptions SHALL be registered before the violation is waived.

Registering an exception after detection is acceptable.

Registering an exception retroactively to conceal a violation is not.

---

# 11. Enforcement Lifecycle

Every enforcement run SHALL follow this sequence.

```text
Trigger

↓

Collect Artifacts

↓

Layer 0: Schema

↓

Layer 1: Structural

↓

Layer 2: Invariant

↓

Layer 3: Semantic

↓

Layer 4: Lifecycle

↓

Score Calculation

↓

Violation Registry Update

↓

Report Generation

↓

Gate Decision (Hard violations present?)

↓

BLOCK or PASS
```

---

# 12. Enforcement Triggers

Enforcement SHALL be triggered by:

* repository initialization (boot);
* capability execution (pre-execution check);
* artifact creation or modification;
* AED promotion (status transition);
* scheduled interval (periodic health check);
* explicit agent or human request.

Enforcement SHOULD be triggered automatically.

Manual enforcement SHALL remain available.

---

# 13. Gate Integration

Enforcement gates SHALL be applied at defined execution points.

---

## Gate: Pre-Execution

Before any Capability executes:

* Layer 0 and Layer 1 SHALL pass.
* Hard violations SHALL block execution.
* Soft violations SHALL be reported but SHALL NOT block.

---

## Gate: Pre-Promotion

Before an AED is promoted from Proposed to Accepted:

* All layers SHALL pass for the promoted document.
* The promoting AED SHALL not introduce new Hard violations in the graph.

---

## Gate: Periodic Health

At the scheduled interval:

* All layers run.
* Score is computed.
* DEGRADED or CRITICAL scores trigger an architectural review process.

---

# 14. Enforcement Contracts

The Enforcement Subsystem is itself a Capability.

It SHALL expose a Contract.

Contract summary:

* Purpose: verify repository governance compliance.
* Inputs: repository artifact set, governance rules, exception registry.
* Outputs: Compliance Score, Violation Registry, Enforcement Report.
* Consumers: agents, humans, CI systems, boot sequence.
* Invariants: see Section 15.
* Failure Conditions: unable to access artifact set; governance rules unavailable.

---

# 15. Enforcement Invariants

The following SHALL always remain true.

---

## ENF-001

Enforcement SHALL be deterministic.

Given the same repository state and governance rules, enforcement SHALL produce the same result.

---

## ENF-002

Enforcement SHALL be non-destructive.

Enforcement SHALL never modify repository artifacts.

---

## ENF-003

Every Hard violation SHALL block the relevant gate.

No Hard violation SHALL be silently suppressed.

---

## ENF-004

Every Soft violation SHALL be recorded.

No Soft violation SHALL be silently discarded.

---

## ENF-005

Every violation SHALL be traceable to a specific governance rule.

Violations without a traceable rule SHALL be considered enforcement defects.

---

## ENF-006

The Compliance Score SHALL be recomputed after every enforcement run.

Stale scores SHALL be treated as governance risk.

---

## ENF-007

Exceptions SHALL be explicit.

Implicit exceptions SHALL be treated as Hard violations.

---

## ENF-008

Enforcement itself SHALL be governed.

The enforcement capability SHALL be subject to the same compliance model as every other capability.

---

## ENF-009

Enforcement SHALL remain implementation-independent.

The compliance model SHALL not depend on any specific tool, runtime, or language.

---

## ENF-010

A CRITICAL Compliance Score SHALL trigger an architectural review.

No repository operating at CRITICAL score SHALL proceed without documented acknowledgement.

---

# 16. Architectural Smells

The following patterns indicate enforcement degradation.

* violations suppressed without registered exceptions;
* exceptions without expiry conditions;
* enforcement runs skipped without justification;
* Compliance Score not exposed in boot sequence;
* Violation Registry treated as runtime artifact;
* Hard violations demoted to Soft without architectural review;
* enforcement logic embedded inside governance documents;
* enforcement capability consuming runtime state as input;
* perpetual exceptions treated as architectural norms;
* manual enforcement replacing automated enforcement systematically.

These SHALL be treated as first-class violations.

---

# 17. Relationship to Governance

Enforcement and Governance are distinct.

Governance defines what SHALL be.

Enforcement verifies what IS.

Enforcement findings flow back into governance as evidence.

Governance evolution MAY be triggered by recurring enforcement patterns.

This creates the repository's only sanctioned feedback loop.

```text
Governance

↓

Enforcement

↓

Violation Registry

↓

Architectural Review

↓

Governance Update (if warranted)

↓

Governance
```

This loop SHALL remain explicit.

Implicit feedback is indistinguishable from drift.

---

# 18. Future Evolution

Enforcement implementation MAY evolve.

Possible implementations include:

* static analysis scripts;
* CI/CD pipeline stages;
* pre-commit hooks;
* MCP-integrated validators;
* agent-native compliance tools;
* vector-indexed semantic checkers;
* graph-based invariant verifiers.

The Compliance Model, Violation Model, and Exception Model SHALL remain stable regardless of implementation.

---

# 19. Decision

This AED establishes Compliance and Enforcement as the operational backbone of the governance system.

Governance without enforcement ends here.

From this point forward:

* every governance rule is either enforceable or explicitly advisory;
* every artifact is either compliant, excepted, or in violation;
* every violation is either open, resolved, or excepted;
* the repository always knows its own compliance state.

---

# 20. Foundational Principle

> **A governance system that cannot measure its own compliance**
> **is indistinguishable from a governance system that does not exist.**

The repository SHALL know, at any moment, whether it is governed.

That knowledge is the output of this subsystem.

Everything else in this AED is implementation detail.
