# AED-00013

# Exception Registry Architecture

---

# Status

**Proposed**

Normative.

---

# 1. Purpose

This AED defines the canonical model for governance exceptions.

AED-00011 introduced the Exception concept and its mandatory fields.

This AED fully specifies the Exception lifecycle, the Exception Registry architecture, governance rules, audit requirements, and the relationship between exceptions and compliance.

---

# 2. Motivation

No governance system is perfectly applicable to every situation.

Exceptions are inevitable.

The danger is not exceptions themselves.

The danger is untracked exceptions.

An untracked exception is indistinguishable from a violation.

An accumulation of untracked exceptions is indistinguishable from governance collapse.

The Exception Registry is the mechanism by which the governance system distinguishes deliberate flexibility from accidental drift.

---

# 3. Core Principle

Exceptions are not failures.

Exceptions are governance decisions made under constraint.

Every exception MUST be explicit.

Every exception MUST be bounded.

Every exception MUST be owned.

An exception that satisfies these three conditions is a legitimate governance instrument.

An exception that violates any of these conditions is a violation.

---

# 4. Exception Definition

An Exception is a formally registered waiver of one or more governance rules for a defined scope and duration.

Exceptions do not suspend governance.

Exceptions record that governance was considered and a conscious decision was made.

---

# 5. Exception Types

Every exception SHALL be assigned one type at registration.

---

## Type 1 — Temporary

A Temporary Exception accommodates a known, time-bounded situation.

Examples:

* a capability under active refactoring that temporarily violates a contract requirement;
* an AED in Draft that references a Published AED being revised simultaneously;
* a dependency cycle introduced by an in-progress architectural migration.

Temporary Exceptions SHALL carry a mandatory expiry date.

Temporary Exceptions SHALL NOT be renewed more than twice without triggering a Structural review.

---

## Type 2 — Structural

A Structural Exception accommodates a permanent or long-lived architectural constraint.

Examples:

* a third-party integration that cannot expose a Contract in the canonical format;
* a legacy artifact that is read-only and cannot be updated;
* an external dependency that violates the dependency direction rule but cannot be replaced.

Structural Exceptions do not have a fixed expiry date.

Structural Exceptions SHALL be reviewed at every major governance Suite Version increment.

Structural Exceptions SHALL be treated as architectural debt until resolved.

---

## Type 3 — Emergency

An Emergency Exception is applied immediately in response to a blocking compliance situation.

Emergency Exceptions allow execution to proceed while a proper resolution is designed.

Emergency Exceptions SHALL be proposed and applied within one operational session.

Emergency Exceptions SHALL be ratified as Temporary or Structural within seven days.

An unratified Emergency Exception automatically becomes a Hard violation after seven days.

---

# 6. Exception Lifecycle

Every Exception SHALL follow this lifecycle.

```text
Proposed

↓

Active

↓

Expired     ←— time-bound (Temporary)
    or
Ratified    ←— converted to Structural after review
    or
Revoked     ←— cancelled before expiry
    or
Resolved    ←— underlying violation corrected; exception no longer needed
```

---

## Proposed

The exception has been submitted but not yet approved.

Proposed exceptions SHALL NOT suppress violations.

Proposed exceptions SHALL be visible in the Violation Registry as pending.

---

## Active

The exception has been approved.

Active exceptions suppress the relevant violation from the Compliance Score calculation.

Active exceptions SHALL appear in the Exception Registry with full detail.

---

## Expired

A Temporary Exception that has passed its expiry date without renewal or resolution.

Expired exceptions SHALL immediately reactivate the suppressed violation.

Expired exceptions SHALL trigger a Soft violation in the Violation Registry.

---

## Ratified

An Emergency Exception that has been reviewed and converted to Temporary or Structural.

Ratification closes the Emergency Exception and opens a new exception of the assigned type.

---

## Revoked

An exception cancelled before its natural expiry.

Revocations are initiated by the owner or by architectural review.

Revoked exceptions SHALL immediately reactivate the suppressed violation.

---

## Resolved

The underlying governance violation has been corrected.

The exception is no longer needed.

Resolved exceptions are closed with a resolution record.

Resolved exceptions preserve the complete exception history.

---

# 7. Exception Record

Every exception SHALL expose the following fields.

---

## Mandatory Fields

* `id` — unique identifier (EXC-NNNNN format)
* `type` — Temporary / Structural / Emergency
* `status` — Proposed / Active / Expired / Ratified / Revoked / Resolved
* `title` — short description
* `violated_rule` — reference to the specific invariant, directive, or constraint being waived
* `affected_entities` — list of entity identifiers impacted by this exception
* `justification` — why the exception is necessary
* `owner` — human accountable for this exception
* `registered_at` — timestamp of registration
* `approved_at` — timestamp of approval (null if Proposed)
* `exit_criteria` — conditions under which the exception can be closed

---

## Conditional Fields

* `expiry_date` — required for Temporary exceptions
* `review_date` — required for Structural exceptions
* `ratification_deadline` — required for Emergency exceptions (registered_at + 7 days)
* `superseded_by` — reference to the exception that replaced this one (if ratified or renewed)
* `resolution_note` — required when status transitions to Resolved

---

## Audit Fields

These fields are system-managed and SHALL NOT be manually modified.

* `compliance_impact` — the Compliance Score delta introduced by this exception
* `violation_suppressed` — reference to the Violation Registry entry being suppressed
* `last_reviewed_at` — timestamp of most recent review
* `renewal_count` — number of times this exception has been renewed (Temporary only)

---

# 8. Exception Registry

The Exception Registry is the canonical store of all exceptions.

---

## Registry Responsibilities

The Exception Registry SHALL:

* enumerate all Active exceptions;
* preserve all historical exceptions (Expired, Revoked, Resolved);
* expose exception impact on the Compliance Score;
* surface exceptions approaching expiry;
* flag Emergency exceptions approaching ratification deadline;
* link every exception to its suppressed violation.

---

## Registry Properties

The Exception Registry is a governance artifact.

It SHALL NOT be treated as runtime state.

The Exception Registry SHALL be versioned.

The Exception Registry SHALL be discoverable through the Repository Registry.

---

## Registry Location

```text
governance/
    exceptions/
        EXCEPTION-REGISTRY.md   ← index of all exceptions
        active/                 ← one file per Active exception
        archive/                ← one file per closed exception
```

---

## Registry Format

The registry index SHALL expose a summary table.

```text
| ID          | Type       | Status   | Violated Rule | Owner   | Expiry     |
| ----------- | ---------- | -------- | ------------- | ------- | ---------- |
| EXC-00001   | Temporary  | Active   | ENF-003       | human   | 2026-09-01 |
| EXC-00002   | Structural | Active   | CON-001       | human   | review     |
| EXC-00003   | Emergency  | Ratified | INV-002       | human   | —          |
```

---

# 9. Exception Governance

Exceptions are governed assets.

---

## Approval Authority

Exceptions SHALL be approved by the artifact owner or architectural authority.

No exception SHALL self-approve.

An exception registered by the same entity that owns the violated rule requires explicit rationale.

---

## Approval Criteria

An exception SHALL be approved when all of the following are true:

* the justification is credible and specific;
* the scope is bounded (not "all capabilities");
* the exit criteria are concrete and testable;
* the owner is identified;
* the compliance impact is declared;
* the type is correctly assigned.

An exception SHALL be rejected when:

* it suspends a Class A invariant without extraordinary justification;
* the exit criteria are "never" or "indefinite" without Structural classification;
* the owner cannot be identified;
* the scope encompasses the entire repository.

---

## Perpetual Exceptions

Exceptions that have been renewed more than twice SHALL be reviewed for Structural reclassification.

Structural exceptions that have persisted beyond two major Suite Version increments SHALL trigger an architectural review.

No exception SHALL exist indefinitely without review.

---

# 10. Exception Debt

Accumulated exceptions represent exception debt.

Exception debt is a governance liability.

Exception debt SHALL be tracked and reported in AED-00014.

---

## Debt Indicators

High exception debt is indicated by:

* more than ten Active Temporary exceptions simultaneously;
* any Temporary exception with more than two renewals;
* any Emergency exception unratified after seven days;
* Structural exceptions not reviewed in the last major Suite cycle;
* total Compliance Score impact of Active exceptions exceeding 0.15.

These indicators SHALL trigger a Soft violation.

---

# 11. Compliance Score Interaction

Active exceptions affect the Compliance Score.

---

## Exception Suppression

An Active exception suppresses the associated violation from Hard count.

However, exception suppression is partial.

The suppressed violation counts as a Soft violation regardless of original mode.

This ensures that exceptions do not create invisible compliance gaps.

---

## Exception Penalty

The Compliance Score calculation SHALL apply an exception penalty.

```text
Exception Penalty = (Active Exception Count × 0.01) + (Expired Exception Count × 0.02)
```

The penalty reflects that exceptions, even legitimate ones, represent governance constraint.

---

# 12. Audit Trail

The Exception Registry SHALL maintain an immutable audit trail.

---

## Audit Events

Every status transition SHALL produce an audit event.

Audit events SHALL record:

* timestamp;
* previous status;
* new status;
* actor;
* rationale.

---

## Immutability

Audit events SHALL NOT be modified after creation.

Audit events SHALL NOT be deleted.

Expired, Revoked, and Resolved exceptions SHALL be preserved in the archive indefinitely.

---

# 13. Exception Report

The enforcement subsystem SHALL generate an Exception Report as part of every enforcement run.

The Exception Report SHALL include:

* count of Active exceptions by type;
* count of Expired exceptions since last run;
* exceptions approaching expiry in the next 14 days;
* Emergency exceptions approaching ratification deadline;
* total exception penalty applied to Compliance Score;
* exception debt indicators (see Section 10).

---

# 14. Exception Invariants

The following SHALL always remain true.

---

## EXC-001

Every registered exception SHALL be traceable to a specific violated rule.

Generic exceptions (e.g., "some governance rules may be violated") SHALL NOT be accepted.

---

## EXC-002

Every Active exception SHALL have an identified owner.

Ownerless exceptions SHALL be treated as invalid.

---

## EXC-003

Every Temporary exception SHALL have an expiry date.

Temporary exceptions without expiry dates SHALL be treated as invalid.

---

## EXC-004

Emergency exceptions SHALL be ratified or rejected within seven days.

Unratified Emergency exceptions SHALL generate Hard violations after seven days.

---

## EXC-005

Expired exceptions SHALL never silently suppress violations.

An expired exception that is still suppressing a violation SHALL be treated as a governance defect.

---

## EXC-006

Exception audit trails SHALL be immutable.

---

## EXC-007

Exceptions SHALL be approved before they suppress violations.

Pre-emptive self-approval is prohibited.

---

## EXC-008

The Exception Registry SHALL be synchronized with the Violation Registry.

Every suppressed violation SHALL reference its active exception.

Every active exception SHALL reference its suppressed violation.

---

## EXC-009

Class A invariants (Foundational governance) SHALL NOT be excepted without an architectural review.

---

## EXC-010

Exception debt indicators SHALL be surfaced in every enforcement report.

Exception debt SHALL NOT be hidden.

---

# 15. Architectural Smells

The following patterns indicate exception governance degradation.

* exceptions approved without identified owner;
* exceptions with no exit criteria;
* Temporary exceptions renewed indefinitely without Structural reclassification;
* Emergency exceptions never ratified;
* exceptions suppressing violations without registry synchronization;
* exception audit trail containing modifications;
* Compliance Score not reflecting exception penalty;
* exceptions used to avoid governance work rather than accommodate genuine constraints;
* exception registry treated as runtime artifact and lost between sessions;
* exception scope defined as "all entities" or "entire repository."

---

# 16. Future Evolution

The Exception Registry implementation MAY evolve.

Future implementations MAY use:

* structured YAML or JSON exception files;
* database-backed registries;
* MCP-integrated exception workflows;
* automated expiry enforcement;
* agent-assisted exception review.

The exception model SHALL remain unchanged.

---

# 17. Decision

This AED establishes the Exception Registry as a first-class governance subsystem.

Exceptions are no longer informal agreements.

From this point forward:

* every exception is registered before or immediately after it is applied;
* every exception is bounded by scope, duration, and ownership;
* every exception is visible in the Compliance Score calculation;
* no exception is silent.

---

# 18. Foundational Principle

> **An exception that cannot be found is not an exception.**

> **It is a hidden violation.**

The Exception Registry exists to make deliberate flexibility visible.

Visible flexibility is governable.

Invisible flexibility is entropy.
