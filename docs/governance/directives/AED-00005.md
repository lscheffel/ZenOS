# AED-00005

# Governance Architecture

---

# Status

**Proposed**

Normative.

---

# 1. Purpose

This AED defines the Governance Subsystem.

Governance SHALL become an independent architectural subsystem.

It SHALL NOT be treated as documentation.

It SHALL NOT be implemented as prompt engineering.

Governance SHALL behave as the constitutional layer of the repository.

---

# 2. Mission

The purpose of Governance is **not** to execute work.

Its purpose is to guarantee that work is executed consistently.

Governance exists to reduce entropy.

---

# 3. Core Principle

Execution changes.

Governance remains stable.

Repository evolution SHALL prioritize stable governance over stable implementation.

---

# 4. Responsibilities

Governance SHALL:

* define architectural expectations;
* establish operational boundaries;
* define quality standards;
* resolve conflicts;
* preserve consistency;
* minimize ambiguity;
* prevent architectural drift.

Governance SHALL NOT:

* implement features;
* execute workflows;
* store runtime information;
* duplicate project knowledge.

---

# 5. Governance Domains

Governance SHALL be decomposed into independent domains.

Each domain owns one concern only.

---

## Directives

Purpose:

Mandatory behavioral obligations.

Language:

**MUST**

Examples:

* Architecture MUST remain modular.
* Contracts MUST exist.
* Artifacts MUST expose metadata.

Directives define positive obligations.

---

## Constraints

Purpose:

Explicit limitations.

Language:

**MUST NOT**

Examples:

* Capabilities MUST NOT redefine ontology.
* Runtime MUST NOT become documentation.
* Knowledge MUST NOT be duplicated.

Constraints define negative obligations.

---

## Policies

Purpose:

Recommended organizational behavior.

Language:

**SHOULD**

Policies define preferred practices.

Policies MAY be overridden.

Overrides SHOULD be documented.

---

## Standards

Purpose:

Define measurable quality.

Examples:

Naming.

Metadata.

Formatting.

Versioning.

Documentation.

Standards describe quality expectations.

---

## Decisions

Purpose:

Document architectural evolution.

Represented primarily by AEDs.

Decisions preserve rationale.

They do not replace governance.

---

# 6. Governance Hierarchy

The governance system SHALL obey strict precedence.

```text
Repository Vision
        │
Architecture Principles
        │
Directives
        │
Constraints
        │
Policies
        │
Standards
        │
Capabilities
        │
Project Rules
        │
Execution
```

Higher layers SHALL override lower layers.

Lower layers SHALL never redefine higher layers.

---

# 7. Conflict Resolution

Governance SHALL resolve conflicts deterministically.

Priority SHALL follow:

1. Repository Vision
2. Architecture Principles
3. Directives
4. Constraints
5. Policies
6. Standards
7. Project Rules
8. Runtime Decisions

No lower layer SHALL invalidate a higher layer.

---

# 8. Governance Scope

Governance SHALL remain repository-wide.

Project-specific decisions SHALL remain outside Governance whenever possible.

Governance describes the operating system.

Projects describe business implementation.

These concerns SHALL remain separated.

---

# 9. Governance Lifecycle

Governance evolves slower than every other subsystem.

Expected stability:

Vision

↓

Architecture

↓

Governance

↓

Capabilities

↓

Project

↓

Runtime

Frequent governance changes indicate architectural instability.

---

# 10. Governance Consumers

Governance SHALL be consumed by:

* Agents
* Skills
* Commands
* Workflows
* Templates
* Validators
* Reviewers

Governance SHALL NOT require duplication inside those consumers.

---

# 11. Governance Contracts

Every governance artifact SHOULD expose:

Purpose

Authority

Scope

Priority

Dependencies

Exceptions

Version

Review Cycle

Governance SHALL become self-describing.

---

# 12. Repository Constitution

Collectively, the following documents form the constitutional layer.

* Architectural Vision
* AEDs
* Directives
* Constraints

Everything else SHALL derive from these documents.

---

# 13. Governance Invariants

The following SHALL remain true.

---

## GOV-001

Governance SHALL remain centralized.

---

## GOV-002

Governance SHALL remain implementation-independent.

---

## GOV-003

Governance SHALL never depend upon project knowledge.

---

## GOV-004

Governance SHALL never contain runtime state.

---

## GOV-005

Governance SHALL minimize ambiguity.

---

## GOV-006

Governance SHALL reduce entropy.

---

## GOV-007

Governance SHALL be deterministic.

---

## GOV-008

Governance SHALL explain itself.

---

# 14. Architectural Smells

The following patterns indicate governance degradation.

* duplicated rules;
* conflicting directives;
* policies behaving as constraints;
* constraints behaving as policies;
* project-specific governance;
* undocumented exceptions;
* implicit precedence;
* governance embedded inside prompts;
* governance duplicated inside skills;
* governance duplicated inside commands.

These SHALL be progressively eliminated.

---

# 15. Exception Model

Exceptions SHALL be explicit.

Every governance exception SHOULD answer:

* Why?
* Scope?
* Duration?
* Owner?
* Exit Criteria?

Temporary exceptions SHALL remain temporary.

---

# 16. Future Evolution

Governance SHOULD progressively become machine-readable.

Markdown remains the canonical authoring format.

However, governance SHOULD eventually expose structured metadata suitable for:

* validation;
* automated review;
* repository analysis;
* agent reasoning.

Markdown SHALL remain the human interface.

The governance model SHALL remain independent from storage technology.

---

# 17. Decision

This AED establishes Governance as the constitutional subsystem of the repository.

Governance SHALL no longer be interpreted as documentation.

Governance SHALL become an active architectural control system.

Future repository evolution SHALL preserve this distinction.

---

# 18. Constitutional Principle

> **Execution is free to innovate.**
>
> **Governance is responsible for preserving order.**

The repository SHALL optimize for controlled evolution.

Innovation without governance produces entropy.

Governance without innovation produces stagnation.

The architectural objective is sustained evolution through explicit governance.
