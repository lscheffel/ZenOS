# AED-00012

# Governance Change Management Process

---

# Status

**Proposed**

Normative.

---

# 1. Purpose

This AED defines how the governance system evolves.

Governance is not static.

Governance SHOULD improve continuously.

Without a defined change process, governance evolution produces inconsistency.

This AED establishes the canonical process for proposing, reviewing, approving, and retiring governance artifacts.

---

# 2. Motivation

AED-00005 establishes governance as the constitutional layer.

AED-00011 establishes how compliance is verified.

Neither defines how the constitution itself changes.

A governance system that cannot evolve becomes obsolete.

A governance system that evolves without process produces chaos.

The change process is therefore a governance concern.

---

# 3. Core Principle

Governance changes SHALL follow the same rigor as the governance they modify.

A change that weakens compliance without justification SHALL be rejected.

A change that clarifies ambiguity SHALL be prioritized.

A change that eliminates an architectural smell SHALL be encouraged.

---

# 4. Scope

This process applies to all governance artifacts.

Governed artifacts include:

* Architecture Decision Records (AEDs);
* Directives;
* Constraints;
* Policies;
* Standards;
* Ontology entries;
* Invariant definitions;
* Enforcement rules.

Project artifacts are outside this scope.

Capability implementations are outside this scope.

---

# 5. Change Classification

Every proposed change SHALL be classified before review.

---

## Class A — Foundational

Affects AED-00001, AED-00002, or AED-00003.

Foundational changes affect the entire repository architecture.

Class A changes require the highest review rigor.

Class A changes MAY require multiple AEDs to be updated simultaneously.

---

## Class B — Core

Affects AED-00004 through AED-00014 or any future Core AED.

Core changes affect one architectural subsystem.

Class B changes require formal review and Compliance re-evaluation.

---

## Class C — Operational

Affects policies, standards, or operational directives.

Class C changes affect day-to-day behavior rather than structure.

Class C changes require review but may be approved more rapidly.

---

## Class D — Additive

Introduces a new AED or new governance artifact.

Does not modify existing governance.

Class D changes require review to verify non-duplication and ontological consistency.

---

# 6. Impact Classification

Every proposed change SHALL also declare its impact.

---

## Breaking

Removes, narrows, or inverts a SHALL or MUST NOT rule.

Breaking changes SHALL require explicit justification.

Breaking changes SHALL increment the major version of affected artifacts.

Breaking changes SHALL be announced in the Violation Registry as a planned change before adoption.

---

## Additive

Introduces new SHALL or MUST NOT rules without modifying existing ones.

Additive changes SHALL increment the minor version of affected artifacts.

Additive changes are non-breaking by definition.

---

## Clarifying

Restates existing rules more precisely without changing their meaning.

Clarifying changes SHALL increment the patch version of affected artifacts.

Clarifying changes require the least review overhead.

---

# 7. AED Lifecycle

Every AED SHALL follow this lifecycle.

```text
Draft

↓

Proposed

↓

Review

↓

Accepted

↓

Published

↓

Deprecated

↓

Archived
```

No AED SHALL skip a lifecycle stage.

No AED SHALL transition backward except from Review to Proposed.

---

## Draft

The AED is being authored.

Draft AEDs SHALL NOT be referenced as governance by capabilities.

Draft AEDs SHALL NOT appear in the Registry as active artifacts.

---

## Proposed

The AED is complete and submitted for review.

Proposed is the status of an AED that has been written but not yet evaluated.

Proposed AEDs are normative in intent but not yet in authority.

---

## Review

The AED is under active evaluation.

Review SHALL assess:

* consistency with existing AEDs;
* completeness of invariants;
* absence of ontological conflicts;
* enforceability of SHALL statements;
* impact classification accuracy.

Review MAY result in:

* promotion to Accepted;
* return to Proposed with feedback;
* rejection with documented rationale.

---

## Accepted

The AED has passed review.

Accepted AEDs become normative immediately.

Capabilities SHALL begin consuming Accepted AEDs.

The Compliance Subsystem SHALL begin enforcing Accepted AED invariants.

---

## Published

The AED is stable and widely consumed.

Published AEDs SHALL only be changed through the formal change process.

Minor changes may move directly from Accepted to Published.

Major changes SHALL produce a new AED version.

---

## Deprecated

The AED is being retired.

Deprecated AEDs SHALL expose a superseded_by reference.

Deprecated AEDs SHALL remain readable.

Deprecated AEDs SHALL NOT be enforced as current governance.

A deprecation period SHALL be observed before archiving.

Minimum deprecation period: one major repository revision cycle.

---

## Archived

The AED is retired.

Archived AEDs are preserved for historical reference.

Archived AEDs SHALL NOT be consumed by active capabilities.

Archived AEDs SHALL NOT be deleted.

---

# 8. Change Proposal

Every change begins as a Change Proposal.

---

## Proposal Record

Every Change Proposal SHALL expose:

* Identifier
* Title
* Affected Artifacts
* Change Classification (A / B / C / D)
* Impact Classification (Breaking / Additive / Clarifying)
* Motivation
* Proposed Changes (diff or description)
* Compliance Impact (will this affect the Compliance Score?)
* Backward Compatibility Statement
* Proposed Effective Date

---

## Proposal Source

Change Proposals MAY originate from:

* enforcement findings (recurring violation patterns);
* architectural reviews (triggered by CRITICAL score);
* capability evolution (new execution model not covered by existing AEDs);
* ontological drift detection (semantic enforcement findings);
* scheduled review cycles;
* human architectural decision.

---

# 9. Review Process

Review SHALL evaluate the proposal against the following criteria.

---

## Criterion 1: Necessity

Does the change address a real problem?

Is the problem currently causing governance failures?

---

## Criterion 2: Consistency

Does the change preserve consistency with higher-layer AEDs?

Does the change introduce conflicts with existing invariants?

---

## Criterion 3: Completeness

Does the change fully address the identified problem?

Does the change leave ambiguity that a future AED must resolve?

---

## Criterion 4: Enforceability

Is every new SHALL statement enforceable by Layer 2 (Invariant Enforcement)?

If not, can it be made enforceability via a new invariant?

---

## Criterion 5: Non-Duplication

Does the change duplicate knowledge that already exists?

If so, the duplicate SHALL be removed and a reference SHALL replace it.

---

## Criterion 6: Ontological Coherence

Does the change introduce terminology consistent with AED-00003?

If new terms are introduced, are they added to the Ontology?

---

# 10. Versioning

All governance artifacts SHALL use semantic versioning.

```text
MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH
```

* MAJOR: Breaking change.
* MINOR: Additive change.
* PATCH: Clarifying change.

The governance system itself maintains a Suite Version.

The Suite Version represents the collective version of the governance artifact set.

```text
Governance Suite v1.0.0
```

Breaking changes to any Class A or Class B AED SHALL increment the Suite MAJOR version.

---

# 11. Backward Compatibility

The governance system SHALL minimize breaking changes.

When breaking changes are unavoidable:

* a deprecation notice SHALL be issued;
* a migration path SHALL be documented;
* a deprecation period SHALL be observed;
* affected capabilities SHALL be notified.

Breaking a Contract is more severe than breaking an implementation.

Governance Contracts SHALL therefore be maintained with the highest stability commitment.

---

# 12. Governance Debt

Deferred changes accumulate as governance debt.

Governance debt SHALL be tracked.

Governance debt includes:

* Proposed AEDs not yet reviewed;
* Accepted AEDs not yet Published;
* Deprecated AEDs not yet Archived;
* Change Proposals not yet evaluated;
* Recurring violations not addressed by governance updates.

Governance debt SHALL appear in the Governance Health Metrics (AED-00014).

---

# 13. Emergency Changes

Emergency changes are Breaking changes required to address an active CRITICAL compliance situation.

Emergency changes SHALL be classified as Class A or Class B regardless of scope.

Emergency changes SHALL be applied with abbreviated review.

Emergency changes SHALL be ratified through normal review within seven days of application.

Unratified emergency changes SHALL automatically generate a Hard violation.

---

# 14. Change Invariants

The following SHALL always remain true.

---

## CHG-001

Every change to a governance artifact SHALL follow this process.

No artifact SHALL be modified outside the change process.

---

## CHG-002

Breaking changes SHALL be announced before they take effect.

Silent breaking changes are prohibited.

---

## CHG-003

Every AED promotion SHALL increment the artifact version.

AEDs SHALL NOT be modified without version increment.

---

## CHG-004

Deprecated AEDs SHALL expose a superseded_by reference before archiving.

---

## CHG-005

The Suite Version SHALL be updated whenever any Class A or Class B AED version changes.

---

## CHG-006

Emergency changes SHALL be ratified within seven days.

---

## CHG-007

Rejected Change Proposals SHALL be archived with documented rationale.

Rejected proposals SHALL NOT be deleted.

---

## CHG-008

A Change Proposal that is accepted SHALL produce a traceable link between the proposal and the resulting AED modification.

---

# 15. Architectural Smells

The following patterns indicate change management degradation.

* governance artifacts modified without version increment;
* AEDs promoted without review record;
* deprecated AEDs with no superseded_by reference;
* breaking changes applied silently;
* emergency changes never ratified;
* Change Proposals rejected without documented rationale;
* governance drift addressed by capability workarounds rather than governance updates;
* Suite Version not updated after major AED changes;
* governance debt not tracked;
* review criteria applied inconsistently across change classes.

---

# 16. Future Evolution

The change management process SHALL remain implementation-independent.

Future implementations MAY use:

* pull request workflows;
* agent-assisted review;
* automated consistency checks;
* semantic diffing tools;
* governance CI pipelines.

The process model SHALL remain unchanged.

---

# 17. Decision

This AED establishes the formal Change Management Process for the governance system.

Governance SHALL now evolve under explicit process control.

Informal governance modifications are prohibited from this point forward.

Every change, regardless of size, SHALL leave a traceable record.

---

# 18. Foundational Principle

> **A governance system that cannot govern its own evolution**
> **has already begun to decay.**

The change process is not overhead.

The change process is governance applied to itself.

Without it, the constitution becomes folklore.
